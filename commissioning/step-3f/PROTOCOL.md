## Five-call workflow per arm

Use caller-mediated GPT-5.6 Sol in the same user interface for every arm call; the identification is caller-reported unless independently verifiable. If unavailable before starting, stop and record the deviation. The common order inside each arm is exactly: (1) planning, (2) arrangement generation, (3) failure generation, (4) independent arrangement critique, (5) independent failure critique. This totals thirty experimental intelligence calls across six arms. There are no retries, replacement candidates, rescue calls, extra exploration, post-critique rewrites, or compensating calls. No model call may see the other arm's output. A call failure consumes its slot and is recorded.

Planning receives the exact fixture premise, initial facts, typed keys, all constraint statements and verification classifications, and both aims; ask for exactly two short nonblank intent strings, keyed `arrangement_intent` and `failure_intent`, with no candidate narratives. Ordinary and Eidomancer agents independently choose intents. For the Eidomancer arm, call `forkGeneric` once with two distinct IDs, `B1` and `B2`, using these intents. Both branches start from the identical initial state. Use engine budgets `{calls:2,branches:2,depth:1}`; evaluation calls do not debit the engine's generation-call budget.

Each generation call uses fresh context and receives the exact fixture materials, both aims, its corresponding aim and planner-selected intent, and its branch's current state. Request that the intelligence consider possible alternatives internally and return its best coherent candidate, with only the two-field strict JSON object described above. Do not supply the other branch's candidate. The ordinary arm receives these data as plain world and state information without engine IDs, Crystal terminology, or lineage machinery. For Eidomancer, the actual captured, canonical `eidomancer.generic.generation-request.v1` envelope must be the bytes submitted at the adapter boundary; record the exact visible interface submission as well, and distinguish any interface wrapping from what the adapter actually captures. It resolves immutable seed/current-state/rule/constraints/operation context. Do not claim visibility into provider-side transformations. The two generation responses feed the existing strict extraction and mechanical validation; do not silently repair malformed JSON or incomplete maps.

The two independent critique calls each receive fresh context, the exact fixture materials, both aims, the applicable aim and selected intent, the applicable raw candidate and facts, and its numeric-check results (if any). Direct receives an equivalent plain-language check report calculated from the declared validators without engine identifiers. Each critique addresses the applicable narrative constraints explicitly, coherence, causal depth, non-obvious consequences not supplied by the premise, substantive branch diversity insofar as one branch permits judgment, internal consistency, descendant usefulness, weaknesses, uncertainty, and counterexamples. Return one complete JSON object with `verdict` (`retain`, `limited`, `reject`, or `retain_negative_finding`), `rationale` (string), `uncertainty` (string), `limitations` (array of strings), and `judgment` (string); no additional fields or aggregate creativity score. Critics see only their own branch, so final branch diversity is reviewed across both after the arm. Generic captured evaluation must use caller-supplied exact request bytes and a separate evaluator adapter; its evaluator response must be complete and strict. Preserve exact bytes and the evaluation extraction record.

If an Eidomancer generation response is malformed, there is no engine transition/evaluation; spend its already allocated critique call assessing its raw output outside engine evaluation, labeling this limitation explicitly. If a mechanical check fails, the engine produces no valid successor; evaluate the rejected transition, name its actual failed constraint in a generic Crack, and audit as rejected/unresolved according to the critique. In the direct arm apply the same numeric checks and report failures with no engine provenance claim. A narrative violation can cause critique rejection even when a mechanically accepted successor exists. Preserve invalid candidates and their available evidence in both arms.

## Selection, Crystal, and review products

Retain a mechanically valid candidate only when critique supports a coherent, consequential result (`retain` or a sufficiently supported `limited` verdict). Reject or prune weak/invalid results. A defensible failed mechanical boundary may be recorded as `retain_negative_finding` but cannot become a valid successor or be retained as one. Create a generic Crystal only if at least one evaluated valid successor is retained as useful; reference actual immutable transition, evaluation, audit, and session records, and verify required-reference closure. If none qualify, record that no Crystal was created. A Crystal is not itself a substantive advantage in the blind comparison. There is no post-critique regeneration or revision in either arm; descendants and synthesis of revised outcomes are outside this experiment's engine profile.

For each world, prepare two separately and randomly labeled output sets, with an independently recorded assignment key. Each set includes both complete candidate descriptions and fact maps, their full substantive critiques and weaknesses, and selection/rejection outcomes stated in neutral language. Preserve failed candidates. Remove engine request IDs, hashes, audit/provenance labels, Crystal existence, and other procedural identifiers while retaining substance. Keep the X/Y-to-arm key separate until both reviews are frozen. Do not allow reviewers access to call order, source transcript metadata, or another arm's provenance. Because participants mediating calls may recognize outputs, use independent fresh-context blind reviewers rather than treating the mediator's opinion as blinded.

Two independent fresh-context blind reviewers receive each world premise, facts and constraints, aims, and paired anonymized products. They record qualitative comparisons with evidence, weaknesses, and counterexamples along six fixed substantive dimensions: constraint coherence; causal depth; non-obvious consequences not supplied by premise; substantive branch diversity rather than paraphrase; internal consistency; usefulness for descendant exploration. Do not make a single aggregate creativity score. Freeze each review before revealing the arm key. If reviewers materially disagree and cannot support the same conclusion from evidence, mark that world indeterminate. Reviewer calls, if made through GPT-5.6 Sol, are six **separate post-arm calls**, never arm generation/critique calls, and are tracked separately from the equal-budget comparison. After substantive judgments are frozen, reveal the keys and compare provenance/recoverability, Crystal closure, and resource observations separately; these cannot determine the substantive winner.

## Cross-world outcome criteria

- **Support for recurrence:** defensible Eidomancer substantive advantage in at least two of three worlds, without comparable coherence loss or material resource asymmetry explaining the advantages. One exceptional world cannot suffice.
- **Negative evidence:** ordinary agent reaches comparable or better substantive territory with no greater observed opportunity/effort in at least two worlds, or Eidomancer primarily produces redundant branches there. A tie counts only if comparability and resource evidence support this reading.
- **Mixed or ambiguous:** every other pattern, including a single win, opposing world wins, indeterminate reviewer disagreement, consequential invalid branches, or resource/transport asymmetry that makes comparison unfair. Report any world-specific win or failure even when the overall outcome is mixed.

One three-world run is diagnostic, not proof of general superiority, inferiority, or repeatability across arbitrary fictional worlds. Do not retroactively change classification to favor an architecture.
